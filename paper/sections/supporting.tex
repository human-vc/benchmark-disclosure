\section{Additional Checks}\label{sec:supporting}

\paragraph{Is window composition a provider's choice?}
Section~\ref{sec:core-recentring} conditions on window composition and notes that doing so is
defensible only if composition is settled before a provider decides what to report. Regressing the
share of a cell's window released after the focal model on provider dummies gives an $R^2$ of
0.060 across 46 organisations, against 0.024 for benchmark dummies over 62 benchmarks and 0.036
for the release date alone. Window composition is close to idiosyncratic at the cell level, which
is consistent with its being set by the evaluator's scoring schedule rather than by the provider,
and is not enough to establish it.

\paragraph{Is the evaluator's coverage selective?}
The estimand conditions on the cells the evaluator scores, so coverage is upstream of everything
else. Coverage averages 8.7 benchmarks per release with a standard deviation of 8.0 and a range of
1 to 38. It correlates with a provider's release count at 0.203 and with the release date at 0.265,
and releases from the densest quartile of providers carry 9.6 scored benchmarks against 8.4 for the
rest. Coverage is therefore mildly tilted toward larger and later providers, and nothing in this
paper corrects for that.

\paragraph{Bounding the unidentified component.}
Write $p$ for a release date, $c$ for a benchmark's vintage and $a = p - c$ for the benchmark's
maturity when the model met it. A regression of standing on all three is singular by construction,
which is the age-period-cohort problem, and the data identify only
$\Pi_p = \beta_a + \beta_p$ and $\Pi_c = \beta_c - \beta_a$, leaving a one-parameter family indexed
by $s = \beta_a$. Fitting the reduced form on the 2,831 comparable cells gives
$\Pi_p = +4.12$ and $\Pi_c = -1.36$ percentile points per year.

Three restrictions come from the paper rather than from outside it. Capability rises over calendar
time, so $\beta_p \geq 0$. Later benchmarks are less saturated, which
Section~\ref{sec:core-boundary} measures directly, so $\beta_c \leq 0$. Standing rises with
maturity, since a model on a young benchmark is ranked against newer peers, so $\beta_a \geq 0$.
Together these give $0 \leq s \leq \min(\Pi_p, -\Pi_c) = 1.36$, and saturation rather than
capability is the binding restriction. The mean maturity gap between eligible and placebo cells is
2.17 years, so the unidentified component can account for at most $1.36 \times 2.17 = 2.94$
percentile points, 24\% of the observed gap. Adding centred quadratic and cubic terms in $p$ and
$c$, which are not collinear with $a$, moves the ceiling to 34\% and 31\%.

Shuffling scores within benchmark leaves every date and the rank deficiency untouched while
destroying the capability trend. Under that null the same quantity falls to 0.02 percentile points
on average over thirty draws, with a maximum of 0.24, so the bound reflects the trend the geometry
multiplies rather than the geometry alone.

\paragraph{The boundary as a discontinuity.}
Eligibility is $\mathbf{1}[t_i > \tau_b]$, a deterministic function of the two dates, and that is what
the global repairs run into. Equating across groups that took different instruments assumes a
conditional relationship common to both, which two groups defined by the boundary cannot have
\citep{sinharay2010missing}, and reweighting by the probability a cell is observed needs that
probability bounded away from zero, which a deterministic rule denies \citep{ma2019missing}. The
same determinism makes the boundary a sharp discontinuity, where standing at the threshold is
identified under continuity whatever happens globally \citep{hahn2001identification}. Local linear estimates with a triangular kernel
and provider-clustered errors give $-8.82$ (4.76) at $\pm 60$ days, $-6.85$ (4.09) at $\pm 90$,
$-4.08$ (3.11) at $\pm 180$ and $-2.85$ (2.35) at $\pm 250$, and a quadratic fit at each width moves
none of them across zero. All eight are negative, all eight intervals cover zero, and the largest
upper bound is $+8.05$, below the $+12.23$ the global comparison reports.

The local randomization framework agrees \citep{cattaneo2015randomization}. Widening symmetric
windows and testing the release's benchmark count, its scaffold count and its scaffold spread for
balance, the smallest of the three $p$-values is 0.335 at $\pm 20$ days and 0.571 at $\pm 40$ before
falling to 0.058 at $\pm 60$, which selects $\pm 40$. That window holds 113 postdating and 114
eligible cells, and the difference in mean standing is $-2.52$ with a randomization $p$ of 0.447.
Cell counts in the 30 days either side of the cutoff stand at 80 against 84, a ratio of 0.95, so the
running variable is not bunching against the threshold \citep{mccrary2008manipulation}.

The mechanism is discontinuous where standing is not. The share of a cell's window released after
the focal model jumps $+0.077$ at the cutoff, which at the eligible-cell gradient of $-29.09$ implies
about $-2.2$ percentile points, inside every interval above. Peer count and raw score are continuous.
The global gap therefore lives in the 793 days that separate the two groups' mean maturity rather
than in anything happening at the boundary itself.
