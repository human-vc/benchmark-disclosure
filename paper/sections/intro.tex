\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

When a frontier model ships, its provider publishes a card carrying benchmark results, and independent evaluators run standardised suites over the same models \citep{liang2022helm,epoch2026hub}. This paper asks not whether a published figure is accurate but which available figures get published, the older economic question. A reader sees a subset without the set and must price the silence, and disclosure unravels only when the sender's evidence is commonly known \citep{grossman1981,milgrom1981}. \citet{dye1985} shows what breaks that: a receiver who cannot tell a sender holding nothing from one holding something bad pools the two, and withholding survives. Any study of what was left out inherits that pooling, since the object they need is the endowment, the results a sender held when it wrote, which earlier work does not observe.

Benchmark documentation is one of the few settings where that endowment can be bounded from outside. Every benchmark has a publication date, so one published after a model shipped cannot have been in that model's endowment, whatever a provider ran privately. Dating the instruments bounds the endowment from above and splits absence into a result held back and a result that never existed.

Our first contribution is a panel built around that bound. From a pinned snapshot of an independent benchmarking hub taken on 17 August 2026, covering 819 model-versions, we collapse scaffold variants to the release a provider ships, obtaining 353 releases across 46 organisations, 74 benchmarks and 3,082 release-benchmark pairs with an independent score. Every benchmark carries a verified release date, splitting the panel into 2,355 eligible pairs and 476 the provider could not possibly have disclosed. Disclosure is coded with an instrument adapted from clinical outcome reporting bias \citep{kirkham2010,chan2004,dwan2013}, which records what a document says with its source and assigns categories by rule.

Bounding the endowment is not enough, since judging what a sender withheld requires a measure of how those results stood against peers. Comparing standing on available but unreported cells against standing on cells that postdate the release is a falsification test, since both are non-disclosures, and the test fails. Benchmarks that already existed when a model shipped outscore benchmarks built afterwards by 12.23 percentile points on average, positive in 78.1\% of the 146 releases holding both kinds of cell, and nothing in that calculation knows which benchmarks a provider reported. The most favourable measure of standing we can construct still leaves 8.69 points. The contamination is structural, because the date identifying the endowment also shifts the measure of standing used to price silence, leaving availability mechanically correlated with the outcome used to judge absence \citep{heckman1979}.

Because that percentile is ours, the third piece of evidence comes from outside. HELM Lite republishes its full accuracy table at every versioned release behind a pool-relative headline. Across fourteen releases, 24 models appear every time and their 240 published scenario scores are bit-identical, while the pool grows from thirty models to ninety-one. Every one of those headline values moves and five pairs reverse order. A companion leaderboard from the same team, behind an absolute headline, shows none of this.

We report no estimate of how much selective disclosure occurs. No disclosure coding stands behind this version, so nothing here rests on a disclosure label.

\subsection{Related literature}\label{sec:related}

That reporting practice in model documentation is uneven, incomplete and shaped by the sender is established, and we concede it \citep{singh2025leaderboard,ghosh2026evalcards,staufer2025auditcards,kuehnert2026disclosure,zhang2026positional}. None of it conditions on availability, the single thing we add: without a verified benchmark date, an omission and a nonexistence are the same observation. The economics runs from unraveling under a known endowment \citep{grossman1981,milgrom1981} to its failure under endowment uncertainty \citep{dye1985} and under costly disclosure \citep{verrecchia1983}, frictions this design does not separate. The report-card literature supplies the cautionary cases \citep{dranove2010,hotz2013,jin2003}, including withholding by high-quality senders \citep{bederson2018}, whose sign runs opposite to the expected direction. Concurrent theory models strategic evaluation and persuasion directly \citep{truong2026strategic,dai2026persuasion}; that work is theoretical, this paper is measurement, and nothing here bears on it.
