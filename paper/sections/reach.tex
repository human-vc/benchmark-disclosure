\section{Coding the Record}\label{sec:reach}

A benchmark can be missing from a model card because it was irrelevant, never run, or run and withheld. Only the third is disclosure in the sense of \citet{grossman1981}, \citet{milgrom1981} and \citet{dye1985}, and only it is a reader's to price. The within-family drop narrows it: a provider that reports $b$ at $t$ and omits it at $t+1$, an independent score present, has established relevance and possession of the harness by its own earlier behaviour, with the independent score as the registry-style counterfactual \citep{chan2004,dwan2013,kirkham2010}. It does not establish that $b$ was run for $t+1$, so the \citet{dye1985} uncertainty narrows rather than closes. Its ceiling, 37 pairs, and detection threshold, roughly 38 points at one drop, sit in Appendix~\ref{sec:supporting}.

A single coder read the release-time document of each of the 100 releases for which one is locatable; eight of the queued 108 have none. The record notes every benchmark a document reports, with variants, bare mentions, and stated benign absences; the ORBIT categories are derived mechanically from these records and the family history. This yields 1,280 coded cells, of which 31 belong to five later-vintage releases outside the pinned panel and are excluded. An independent second coder then recoded a provider-stratified fifth of the releases, blind to the first coding and without calibration contact; Appendix~\ref{sec:supporting} records the one release where that blindness broke. Agreement is Cohen's $\kappa$ over the derived cells \citep{cohen1960}, with intervals from resampling releases. The two codings agree at $0.95$ $[0.80, 1.00]$ on the primitive judgment of whether a benchmark was reported, at $0.85$ $[0.72, 0.95]$ over the full category set, and at $0.81$ $[0.48, 1.00]$ on the high-versus-low-suspicion split. The single E cell each coding derives is the same cell, and Appendix~\ref{sec:supporting} reports the confusion structure. Among the 1,249 eligible coded cells that resolve in the panel, 76.9\% report no number, and the release-level mean is 73.1\% $[65.9, 79.2]$. The rate is descriptive and is not, by itself, evidence of selection.

\autoref{tab:coding} is the section's result: the disclosed-versus-omitted comparison returns $+8.7$ points $[4.1, 13.9]$ over 63 releases; its identifying counterpart, omitted-eligible against postdating cells over the 71 releases holding both, returns $+11.4$ $[5.6, 17.0]$, significant against zero at $p = 0.003$. Read against zero it would be concealment; read against Section~\ref{sec:core} it is the availability gap: computed on the same releases with no labels, the contrast sits at $+14.1$, and under every measure and window the coded gap falls 1.9 to 2.7 points short of its matched counterpart. The shortfall is a point-estimate decomposition, the 23\% reported share times the disclosed-omitted gap, so the labels change this contrast only through composition; intervals spanning eleven points leave a strategic component of several points undetected rather than excluded. The naive reading is robust to two-way provider-and-benchmark errors, to reclassifying every variant cell as an omission, and to any leave-one-out, because the availability gap is. What survives instead is composition. Reported benchmarks stand 8.7 points above omitted eligible ones within release, a composition gap larger than all 9,999 count-preserving random assignments ($p = 0.0001$, the floor the draws allow; null standard deviation 1.9) and one the availability gap cannot produce. Benchmark identity does not carry it: with benchmark fixed effects beside the release effects the coefficient rises from $+7.4$ to $+9.3$, and conditioning on the window share leaves $+7.4$. It does not identify whether standing drove the reporting: relevance, salience, and within-eligible contamination can move disclosure and performance together. The drop estimator realises 16 drops across 15 releases and returns $+2.9$, sign-flip $p = 0.25$ over five providers, the underpowered null its calibration priced. Appendix~\ref{sec:supporting} adds three unrepaired limits: unlocatable documents, coverage that may follow provider emphasis, and the best-across-scaffolds rule.
