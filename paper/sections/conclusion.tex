\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We set out to measure how much of what providers could report they choose not to, and we do
not report that number. The reason is the result. Dating the instruments bounds the endowment
from above, which is the step the disclosure literature has long wanted, but the same dates
that decide whether a benchmark could have been reported also decide where a model sits
inside that benchmark's coverage. Standing against contemporaries moves with that position,
so the variable identifying what a provider could have held is also the variable that moves
the measure of what it withheld.

Two things follow. A measure of standing used to price silence
has to be defended as free of the timing that decides disclosability, and reweighting the window is a partial defence at best. And the within-family drop, the one
comparison that isolates withholding, exists at most 37 times in the current record. Whether that record grows is a reporting-standard
question rather than an estimation one.
