\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We set out to measure how much of what providers could report they choose not to. No cell in this
version is coded, so we do not report that number, and the design that would deliver it has a second
problem that is the result here. Dating the instruments bounds the endowment
from above, which is the step the disclosure literature has long wanted, but the same dates
that decide whether a benchmark could have been reported also decide where a model sits
inside that benchmark's coverage. The variable identifying what could have been held also moves
the measure of what was withheld.

Two things follow. A measure of standing used to price silence
has to be defended as free of the timing that decides disclosability, and reweighting the window is a partial defence at best. And the within-family drop, the comparison that narrows
endowment uncertainty furthest, exists at most 37 times in the current record. Whether that record grows is a reporting-standard
question rather than an estimation one, and two levers sit there. A scale estimated jointly across
benchmarks rather than against whichever peers are present is the direction the leaderboard
evidence points \citep{epoch2026rosetta}, though fitting one on this panel shrinks the placebo gap
rather than closing it, which puts it alongside the other partial repairs.
And since reporting a benchmark once shows a provider holds the harness, carrying a set across a
family's releases removes the empty-endowment excuse and narrows the pooling
\citet{dye1985} identifies, without requiring new disclosure.
