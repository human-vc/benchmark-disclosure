\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We set out to measure how much of what providers could report they choose not to. The coded record returns the number and the reason to distrust it: $+11.4$ points, rejecting a null of zero, yet short of its matched label-free counterpart by only what composition implies, and too imprecise to exclude a strategic component of a few points. Dating the instruments bounds the endowment, though the bound is an assumption about access, and the same dates fix where a model sits inside a benchmark's coverage: what identifies the endowment moves the measure used to price silence, and reweighting is a partial defence. Growing the drop record, 16 drops against 37 qualifying pairs, is a reporting-standard question, answerable in two ways: a jointly estimated scale \citep{epoch2026rosetta}, which shrinks the placebo gap without closing it, and carrying a benchmark set across a family's releases, narrowing the empty-endowment excuse and the \citet{dye1985} pooling at no disclosure cost.
