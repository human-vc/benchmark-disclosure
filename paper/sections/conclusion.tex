\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We set out to measure how much of what providers could report they choose not to. No cell is
coded, so we do not report that number; the design that would deliver it has a second problem,
and that problem is the result. Dating the instruments bounds the endowment from above, the step the
disclosure literature has long wanted, but the same dates decide where a model sits inside a
benchmark's coverage. The variable identifying what could have been held also moves
the measure of what was withheld.

Two things follow. A measure of standing used to price silence
must be defended as free of the timing that decides disclosability, and reweighting is a partial defence at best. And the within-family drop, the comparison that narrows
endowment uncertainty furthest, exists at most 37 times in the current record. Whether that record grows is a
reporting-standard question, and two levers sit there. A scale estimated jointly across benchmarks rather
than against whichever peers are present is the direction the leaderboard evidence points
\citep{epoch2026rosetta}, though on this panel it shrinks the placebo gap rather than closing
it, joining the other partial repairs.
And since reporting a benchmark once shows the harness exists, carrying a set across a family's
releases removes the empty-endowment excuse and narrows the \citet{dye1985} pooling, without
requiring new disclosure.
