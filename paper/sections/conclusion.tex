\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We set out to measure how much of what providers could report they choose not to. The coded record returns the number and the reason to distrust it: $+11.4$ points, significant against zero, yet short of its matched label-free counterpart by only what composition implies, and too imprecise to exclude a strategic component of a few points. Dating the instruments bounds the endowment, though the bound remains an assumption about access, and the same dates decide where a model sits inside a benchmark's coverage: what identifies the endowment moves the measure used to price silence, and reweighting is a partial defence at best. Growing the drop record, 16 drops against a ceiling of 37, is a reporting-standard question with two levers: a jointly estimated scale \citep{epoch2026rosetta}, which shrinks but does not close the placebo gap, and carrying a benchmark set across a family's releases, which narrows the empty-endowment excuse and the \citet{dye1985} pooling at no disclosure cost.
