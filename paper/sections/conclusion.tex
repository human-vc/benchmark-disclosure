\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We set out to measure how much of what providers could report they choose not to. The coded record returns the number and the reason to distrust it. The contrast is $+11.4$ points, rejecting zero yet short of its matched label-free counterpart by what composition implies, and too imprecise to exclude a strategic component of a few points. Three claims separate: non-reporting is extensive, composition is performance-related, and withholding is not established, every possession-grounded test returning null, absent, or opposite-signed evidence. Dating the instruments bounds the endowment; the bound is an assumption about access. The same dates fix where a model sits inside a benchmark's coverage: what identifies the endowment moves the measure pricing silence; reweighting is a partial defence. Growing the 16-drop record is a reporting-standard question: a jointly estimated scale \citep{epoch2026rosetta} shrinks the placebo gap without closing it, and carrying a benchmark set across releases narrows the empty-endowment excuse and the \citet{dye1985} pooling.
